About

PHY321, Introduction to quantum mechanics. Department of Physics, Faculty of Science.

The course

The course starts from an experiment, single-photon interference, and builds the formalism step by step: states, measurement, time evolution, composite systems, continuous space, harmonic oscillator.

  • Chapter 1 · Quantum states

    Single-quantum interference, probability amplitudes, superposition and decoherence, the qubit and the Bloch sphere.

    Chapter 1 questions

  • Chapter 2 · Measurement and operators

    The Stern–Gerlach experiment, linear and Hermitian operators, Pauli matrices, commutators, incompatibility and uncertainty.

    Chapter 2 questions

  • Chapter 3 · Quantum dynamics

    The six postulates, the Schrödinger equation, stationary states, precession and Rabi oscillations.

    Chapter 3 questions

  • Chapter 4 · Multi-qubit systems and entanglement

    Tensor product, Bell states, Bell inequalities, density operator, von Neumann entropy, teleportation, BB84.

    Chapter 4 questions

  • Chapter 5 · Wave function and continuous space

    Wave function, momentum representation and Fourier transform, position and momentum operators, wave packet.

    Chapter 5 questions

  • Chapter 6 · Quantum harmonic oscillator

    Energy quantisation, ladder operators and Fock states, coherent states.

    Chapter 6 questions

Prerequisites

  • Linear algebra: vectors, matrices, eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
  • Calculus: derivatives, integrals, linear differential equations, complex numbers.
  • Physics: classical mechanics, waves and oscillations.

This quiz

The question bank covers six chapters.

  • Each question points to a section of the lecture notes and to the matching section of the Notebook.
  • Eight formats: multiple choice, true or false, calculation, matching, sorting, labelled diagram, flashcard, interpretation. The last two are self-assessed.
  • Two modes: practice (correction after each answer) and exam (timed, marked at the end).
  • No account. Your results stay in your browser. After a first visit, the quiz works offline.

Bibliography

  • C. Cohen-Tannoudji, B. Diu, F. Laloë, Quantum Mechanics, volumes I and II, Wiley-VCH (original French edition: Hermann / EDP Sciences).

    The standard reference, complete and detailed.

  • D. J. Griffiths, D. F. Schroeter, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics, Cambridge University Press.

    Gradual, with many exercises.

  • J. J. Sakurai, J. Napolitano, Modern Quantum Mechanics, Cambridge University Press.

    Begins with Stern–Gerlach and the Dirac formalism, as this course does.

  • R. P. Feynman, R. B. Leighton, M. Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, vol. III.

    The probability-amplitude approach.

  • M. A. Nielsen, I. L. Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information, Cambridge University Press.

    For qubits, entanglement and their applications.
